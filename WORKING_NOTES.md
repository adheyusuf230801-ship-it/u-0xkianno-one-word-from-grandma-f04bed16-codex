## Scoring

Each criterion receives 1–5 points, with 5 strongest:

1. **Fit:** believable as a single word Grandma would text about AI.
2. **Voice:** warm, a little baffled, and natural in a text to a grandchild.
3. **Humor:** the joke works within the word itself.
4. **AI tie:** recognizable connection to AI, robots, or machines.
5. **Freshness:** an unexpected choice that remains readable.

The weighted comparison uses 5 × Fit + 4 × Voice + 3 × Humor + 2 × AI tie
+ Freshness (maximum 75). This favors the earlier criteria; the final decision
also checks them in their stated order rather than letting novelty rescue a
poor fit. All candidates below are single alphabetic tokens under 30 characters.

## Pick: ChattyPT

The word keeps enough of ChatGPT's shape to suggest its subject immediately.
“Chatty” sounds like an affectionate description Grandma might use for someone
who always has something to say. The missing G makes it feel like a remembered
name typed slightly wrong, while the internal capitals retain the recognizable
technology-name shape. It is readable without a caption or an elaborate family
backstory.

ChattyGPT is more exact but feels more like an intentional product pun. Robut is
direct but lacks warmth. The human-name candidates are charming only after the
reader works out the context. ChattyPT offers the strongest combined fit, voice,
and immediate joke. This is an original draft for this submission; no claim is
made that nobody elsewhere has ever independently used the same spelling.

The final output is exactly `ChattyPT`: 8 Unicode characters and 8 UTF-8 bytes,
with no punctuation, spaces, line separators, or trailing newline. The program
uses one fixed constant and does not read a clock, use randomness, or access the
network. Local verification uses repository-local output directories.

### Stage B selection: keep ChattyPT

Rechecking the incumbent on the same scale gives Fit 5, Voice 5, Humor 5,
AI tie 5, Freshness 4: **74/75**. The humor score is the most subjective part:
the joke is gentle, and a reader unfamiliar with ChatGPT may miss it. Its
freshness is not perfect because “chatty” is an obvious association with chat.
Nevertheless, the affectionate description and slightly wrong product name
work together immediately, without inventing a family backstory.

The best challengers, Thankyoubot and Clankypants at 68/75, each lose something
material: Thankyoubot is believable and warm but has a weaker standalone joke;
Clankypants is funnier as a nickname but less natural as a text about chat AI.
Geepeety offers a plausible new typo but less warmth. None beats the incumbent
across the stated criteria, so Stage B honestly retains **ChattyPT** (8 characters,
8 UTF-8 bytes). No README change is needed; its quoted word already matches.
